# The Problem

## Why is this a problem?

Reading something doesn't mean you'll remember it. Real retention comes from
active recall and spaced review, but building that from raw material — a
textbook chapter, a lecture PDF, a set of slides — is tedious: pulling out
the key ideas, writing questions that actually test understanding, grading
open-ended answers, and scheduling review at the right intervals. Most
students skip this step entirely and just re-read or highlight, which feels
productive but doesn't build durable recall. Most tools that do help only
cover one piece of the pipeline (notes, or flashcards, or quizzes) rather
than turning a document into a complete, trackable study loop.

## Who experiences it?

- **Students** studying from PDFs, lecture notes, or textbook chapters who
  want to actually retain the material, not just skim it before an exam.
- **Teachers and instructors** who want to assign readings with real
  comprehension checks, but don't have time to hand-write question sets or
  grade free-text answers for every student.
- **Self-learners** working through technical or academic material on their
  own, without anyone to quiz them or track their progress.

## Why does it matter now?

Study materials increasingly arrive as PDFs — scanned readings, exported
slides, downloaded papers — and students are expected to self-manage more of
their own learning, especially outside structured classroom settings. At the
same time, AI models capable of understanding a document, generating varied
question types, and grading free-text answers with real feedback are now
accessible enough to build this into an ordinary web app rather than a
research project. That combination — more self-directed studying, plus
AI capable of automating the tedious parts of it — makes a tool like this
newly practical.

## What are we trying to change?

We want going from "I have a PDF" to "I have notes, practice questions, and a
review schedule" to take seconds, not hours. Cornote aims to make active
recall and spaced repetition the default way people study from any document,
by automating the parts that currently require the most manual effort:
extracting structured notes, generating varied and well-graded questions, and
building the review loop around them.
