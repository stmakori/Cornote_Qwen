# The Problem

## Why is this a problem?

Reading a PDF is not the same as studying it. Real retention comes from active
recall and spaced review, not from re-reading or highlighting, but building an
active recall workflow from a raw lecture PDF is tedious by hand: pulling out the
key ideas, writing questions that actually test understanding, grading open ended
answers honestly, and coming back to review at the right intervals.

For students in Kenya specifically, three things compound this:

- **Passive reading.** Students receive dense PDF lecture notes with no
  structured way to engage with them actively, so rote memorization dominates.
- **No structured notes.** The Cornell method, splitting a page into cues,
  notes, and a summary, is virtually unknown in most Kenyan classrooms.
  Students either copy notes out by hand or highlight PDFs aimlessly, and both
  are proven to hurt retention.
- **Distraction overload.** Mobile data is precious in Kenya, yet students
  burn it on social media while trying to study. There is no dedicated,
  low bandwidth online study environment built for them.

## Who experiences it?

- **University students in Kenya**, especially at mid to large universities
  such as University of Nairobi, Kenyatta University, Strathmore University,
  USIU, and Moi University, studying STEM, business, law, and pre-med, high
  stakes courses where exam performance directly determines career outcomes.
- **University students across Sub-Saharan Africa** more broadly, facing the
  same PDF heavy study culture and the same lack of structured tools.
- **Teachers and instructors** who want to assign readings with real
  comprehension checks, but do not have time to hand write question sets or
  grade free text answers for every student.
- **Self-learners** working through technical or academic material on their
  own, without anyone to quiz them or track their progress.

## Why does it matter now?

The numbers make the size of this problem concrete:

- Kenya has over **600,000** enrolled university students.
- Sub-Saharan Africa has over **12 million** university enrollments.
- The Africa EdTech market was valued at roughly **$3.5 billion** in 2024.
- An estimated **80%** of students study primarily from PDFs, not textbooks.

At the same time, AI models capable of understanding a document, generating
varied question types, and grading free text answers with real feedback are now
accessible enough to build this into an ordinary web app, not a research
project. No existing product combines automatic Cornell method structuring with
AI grading built specifically for African students studying under these
constraints (limited data, PDF heavy material, high stakes exams). That
combination, more self directed studying plus AI that can automate the tedious
parts of it, makes a tool like this newly practical, and newly needed.

## What are we trying to change?

We want going from "I have a PDF" to "I have notes, practice questions, and a
review schedule" to take seconds, not hours. Cornote aims to make active recall
and spaced repetition the default way Kenyan and African university students
study from any document, by automating the parts that currently require the
most manual effort: extracting structured Cornell notes, generating varied and
well graded questions, verifying that every question is actually supported by
the source material, and building the review loop around them, with four
autonomous Claude agents doing that work continuously in the background rather
than a single one-off AI call.

Every student deserves a smarter way to study.
